How we work

Alberta Unbound turns shared experience into practical power.

We bring together Albertans who are navigating income support, disability benefits, housing, health benefits, and the public systems around them. We share what we learn, make room for disagreement, and turn individual problems into informed, democratic pressure for better outcomes.

01

Dignity, always

No one should have to prove they deserve food, medication, shelter, or a life with room to breathe.

02

Lived experience leads

People navigating these programs are experts. Our stories set the agenda; institutions do not.

03

Knowledge is collective

We document the rules, compare what actually happens, and make the bureaucracy less powerful through shared information.

04

Organized pressure wins

We turn individual complaints into public demands, coordinated calls, and the kind of sustained pressure that breaks rock — and bureaucracies.

How we operate

Open debate. Useful information. No intimidation.

Alberta Unbound is independent and non-partisan. We welcome disagreement about policy, economics, politics, tactics, and the future of Alberta. Viewpoint is not moderated for being unpopular. Threats, discrimination, targeted harassment, doxxing, privacy violations, impersonation, spam, and instructions for serious wrongdoing are conduct problems, not political opinions.

Instruction 01 · Build a campaign

From grievance to organized demand

A campaign is not a list of frustrations. It is a group, a target, a demand, a public action, and a follow-up date.

01

Name the harm

Collect verified experiences and public documents. Remove identifying health, financial, and case details before sharing.

02

Set the demand

Choose one change a decision-maker can grant: reverse a cut, publish a policy, restore an appeal route, or fund a service.

03

Build the crew

Recruit affected people, allies, researchers, storytellers, access workers, and a small coordination team with clear roles.

04

Set the deadline

Announce what you want, by when, and what lawful public action follows if there is no meaningful response.

Instruction 02 · Distribute the work

A campaign crew needs more than a loud voice

Coordinator

Keeps the demand, timeline, meetings, and decisions visible. Does not become the only person who knows what is happening.

Story and evidence lead

Checks facts, gets consent, anonymizes records, and keeps lived experience in the center without turning people into content.

Access lead

Plans captions, seating, transit, scent reduction, quiet space, remote participation, breaks, and plain-language materials.

Outreach lead

Welcomes new people, contacts partner organizations, and creates low-barrier ways to help without requiring public disclosure.

Media lead

Prepares one spokesperson, a short fact sheet, consent-based quotes, and a clear request for coverage.

Care and de-escalation lead

Monitors stress, keeps interactions calm, routes conflict away from participants, and knows when to pause an action.

Instruction 03 · Choose the pressure

The action menu

Match the tactic to the target, your capacity, and the risk to participants. Escalation should increase public pressure—not participant danger.

Delegation

Bring a small group to a decision-maker with one demand, a one-page brief, and a spokesperson. Ask for a response date and publish whether it arrives.

Best for: opening a public record

Petition handoff

Collect consent lawfully, verify the count, and deliver it at a visible public moment with a clear demand and media invitation.

Best for: showing breadth

Teach-in or town hall

Make a complex policy legible through a short briefing, lived experience, questions, and a concrete next step. Record attendance with consent.

Best for: building a base

Lawful picket or rally

Choose a lawful, accessible site; assign marshals, access, care, media, and de-escalation roles; check current bylaws and site rules.

Best for: visible public pressure

Collective media day

Coordinate a release window for verified stories, public records, photos, and a single demand. Protect anonymity and obtain consent before publication.

Best for: changing the narrative

Mutual-aid action

Pair public pressure with practical support: rides, food, document scanning, appointment accompaniment, and accessible meeting space.

Best for: keeping people in the fight

Instruction 04 · Run the action

A one-page action runbook

  1. 1

    Brief everyone

    State the demand, schedule, roles, access plan, media plan, and the stop/pause conditions.

  2. 2

    Keep the message tight

    Repeat the same short demand. Do not improvise accusations or publish unverified claims.

  3. 3

    Document outcomes

    Record who attended, what was delivered, what was promised, and the next date. Get consent before sharing images or names.

After the action

  • Thank participants and check on access or care needs.
  • Publish a factual recap and the decision-maker’s response.
  • Set the next meeting before momentum evaporates.
  • Escalate only after reviewing capacity, risk, and consent.
  • Move individual case details to the Case Power Desk—not the public campaign channel.

Instruction 02 · Build the ladder

Escalate without losing control

Start with one clear demand and a small committed group. Move from private contact to public evidence, then to a lawful public action. Give decision-makers a measurable off-ramp, and set a review date.

Instruction 03 · Use the classic toolkit

Traditional advocacy techniques, adapted for Alberta

Delegations and office visits

Bring 3–8 constituents with one spokesperson, a one-page brief, and a written request. Ask for a response date. Keep the visit calm, public, and accessible.

Petitions and pledge drives

Use one demand, collect consent lawfully, verify signatures, and deliver the result with local media or a public handoff. Track commitments, not just names.

Teach-ins and testimony

Turn confusing policy into a 20-minute briefing with lived experience, verified sources, and a clear next step. Offer captions, remote access, breaks, and plain-language materials.

Pickets and public rallies

Choose a visible, lawful location; assign marshals, accessibility support, a media contact, and a de-escalation lead. Check current bylaws and site rules before gathering.

Boycotts and collective withdrawal

Set a specific goal, communicate the start and end conditions, protect workers and bystanders, and provide alternatives so participation does not punish people already under strain.

Strike and solidarity support

Only pursue workplace action with an established union or qualified labour advice. Build a support plan for food, transport, medication, disability access, and retaliation concerns.

Organizing note: Keep actions peaceful and lawful. Do not threaten, target, doxx, obstruct emergency services, or publish private personal information. Verify event details and accessibility needs before attending.
