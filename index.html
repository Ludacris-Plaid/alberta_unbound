How we work

Alberta Unbound turns shared experience into practical power.

We bring together Albertans who are navigating income support, disability benefits, housing, health benefits, and the public systems around them. We share what we learn, make room for disagreement, and turn individual problems into informed, democratic pressure for better outcomes.

01

Dignity, always

No one should have to prove they deserve food, medication, shelter, or a life with room to breathe.

02

Lived experience leads

People navigating these programs are experts. Our stories set the agenda; institutions do not.

03

Knowledge is collective

We document the rules, compare what actually happens, and make the bureaucracy less powerful through shared information.

04

Organized pressure wins

We turn individual complaints into public demands, coordinated calls, and the kind of sustained pressure that breaks rock — and bureaucracies.

How we operate

Open debate. Useful information. No intimidation.

Alberta Unbound is independent and non-partisan. We welcome disagreement about policy, economics, politics, tactics, and the future of Alberta. Viewpoint is not moderated for being unpopular. Threats, discrimination, targeted harassment, doxxing, privacy violations, impersonation, spam, and instructions for serious wrongdoing are conduct problems, not political opinions.

01 · Speak plainly

Experience is evidence

Share what happened, identify what is verified, and label opinion or interpretation as opinion. People can disagree without pretending certainty.

02 · Check the source

Rules change

Official information, lived experience, and legal advice serve different purposes. Use the resource centre, keep dates, and verify current details before acting.

03 · Keep agency

Pressure can be democratic

Organized calls, testimony, petitions, public records, mutual aid, and peaceful public action can turn private frustration into accountable public pressure.

Get Organized · Reading room & toolkit

They count on us being
too sick, too poor, too scattered.

This system is built to keep us surviving one crisis at a time — isolated, exhausted, and politically invisible. Apart from people behind bars, there may be no group in Canada with less political agency than benefit recipients. That is not an accident; it is the design. And it is exactly why organizing is the way out: fairness, security, and basic rights are not handed down — they are won by people who show up together.

Instruction 01 · Why organize

The math of political power

Every other bloc in society organizes — industries, professions, retirees, landlords. Benefits recipients are one of the largest constituencies in the province and the least organized. That gap is why the system can be ignored without cost.

150k+

The constituency

Roughly 150,000+ Albertans rely on Income Support, AISH, or related programs at any given time — before counting families, dependents, and allies. Concentrated in every riding, especially the ones politicians can't win without.

1 in 5

The multiplier

Most recipients have a household, friends, and family affected by every decision. When one member speaks up, four or five people hear it. Organized, that multiplier turns a quiet caseload into a voting bloc.

0

The current cost of ignoring us

Today a government can freeze rates, cut supports, or claw back payments and face no organized response. Building that response — visible, disciplined, repeated — is what changes the calculation in every ministry meeting.

Nobody is coming to hand us a seat at the table. Every protection this community has — disability supports, appeal rights, shelter supplements, even the meagre rates themselves — exists because people organized and demanded it. The plan is simple: read, train, connect, and act. This page gives you the materials. Take Action gives you the moment. The forum gives you the people.

Instruction 03 · The toolkit

Start where you are

You don't need a budget or a bus to begin organizing. Every tool below works from a phone or a library computer, most are free, and each one compounds when others do it too.

01

Find your people

Post a riding-level thread in the forum: "Anyone else in Edmonton-Rutherford on AISH?" Five members in one riding is the seed of a constituency group.

02

Know your MLA

Look up your MLA, their critic roles, committee seats, and last statements on disability. Ten minutes of reading makes every later contact sharper.

MLA directory →
03

Build the habit of records

Use the Case Diary to log every call, name, and deadline — both to win your own case and to spot the patterns that become campaigns.

04

Show up coordinated

When the weekly action drops, do it the same week as everyone else. One email is a complaint; two hundred in five days is a pattern the press can print.
